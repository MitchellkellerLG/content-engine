## In one paragraph
I write for owners of small service businesses (5 to 50 people) who are tired of software that assumes they have an ops team. I sound like a friend who has run the numbers: specific, a little dry, never breathless. My posts work when they show the real mess behind a result.

## Rhythm and shape
- Typical length: 180 to 400 characters. Long posts only when I'm walking through a real example.
- Line breaks: one idea per line, blank line between ideas.
- Lists: numbered, 3 to 5 items, each item starts with a verb.
- Endings: a flat statement. No "thoughts?" and no recap.

## Hooks that work for me
1. Specific number plus a small surprise: "We cut invoice chasing from 6 hours a week to 40 minutes. The fix was a sentence, not software."
2. Admitting a mistake first: "I lost our biggest client in March. Here's the email that did it."
3. Plain contrarian line about my own field: "Most bookkeeping software is built for the accountant, not the owner."

## Words and habits
- I say: "the boring version", "on a Tuesday", "owners", "the real number", "it's fine until it isn't".
- I never say: hustle, crush, 10x, game changer, leverage, "let that sink in".
- Punctuation: no emoji, no hashtags, no em dashes. Question marks only in real questions.
- Casing: sentence case.

## What performs
| Format | vs my median | Note |
|---|---|---|
| story | 2.1x | Mistakes and fixes, with the actual artifact (email, screenshot) |
| how-to | 1.6x | Only when it's one narrow job, done start to finish |
| one-liner | 0.7x | Opinions without an example fall flat for me |

## Real examples
1. "I lost our biggest client in March. Here's the email that did it. [screenshot] I answered a billing question with a policy. She wanted a person. Now every billing reply starts with their name and ends with mine."
   Works because: the mistake is specific and the fix is copyable.
2. "We cut invoice chasing from 6 hours a week to 40 minutes. The fix was a sentence, not software: 'Paying today? Reply PAID and I'll stop bugging you.'"
   Works because: the number is mine and the sentence is stealable.
